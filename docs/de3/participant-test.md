# Owner-run comparison — 3 to 5 participants

Question: How does a persistent shared surface change what people do with their personal stamp?

Run this after hosted acceptance checks pass. Due date: December 1, 2026, 11:59 p.m. This is a study guide and blank record, not evidence that a participant test has occurred.

## Before the session

Ask permission to observe, take anonymous notes, and capture their marks. Explain that shared marks will remain visible to future visitors and that the prototype has no participant deletion button. Do not collect names or sensitive personal designs. Use participant IDs P1–P5. Do not publish quotes or screenshots without their permission.

Record the board before the first session. Identify any technical test marks already present. Do not describe these as earlier participants. Decide deliberately whether to begin with an empty board or a small documented set of seed marks; that choice affects the experiment. Keep the same board accumulating across sessions and record each participant's starting state.

Alternate task order to reduce the effect of learning the tool: P1/P3/P5 begin in Personal Studio; P2/P4 begin on the Global Board. Give roughly three minutes per destination, allowing more time if they need it. Record the order and time rather than enforcing a score or speed target.

## Neutral prompts

Personal Studio: “Make a stamp and use it on this document. Try it for a little while, and tell me when you feel finished.”

Global Board: “This surface keeps the marks people leave here. Make a stamp, or use the one you made, and leave something if you want to. You can look around first.”

Explain basic gestures only if needed. Do not suggest overlapping, respecting empty space, responding to another mark, or creating a particular pattern. Choosing not to share is a valid observation.

## What to observe

- Do they look at the existing board before designing or placing a mark?
- Do they change the stamp, ink, position, scale, or gesture after seeing another contribution?
- Do they seek empty space, sit beside a mark, overlap it, or ignore it? Record what they did before interpreting why.
- Does permanence affect undo, hesitation, discarding, or the decision to share?
- When do they decide they are finished? Do they mention returning to see later changes?
- Which navigation or interaction moments need explanation?

## Questions afterward

1. What were you thinking about when you chose where to put your mark?
2. Did anything already on the board affect your choices? How?
3. Did knowing it would remain for other people change what you made or how you used it?
4. How did the shared surface feel compared with the private document?
5. Would you want to return? What would you want to see or do?

Avoid assuming the shared version is better. Indifference, avoidance of overlap, a preference for privacy, and confusion about sharing are useful outcomes too.

## After all sessions

Compare the two destinations within each person, then look for patterns and exceptions across people. Note task-order effects, the changing board, small sample size, and the difference between what people said and what they did. Make one justified revision if observations support it, test that revision, and save before/after evidence. Write the final rationale from these actual observations; do not substitute the automated storage tests for audience testing.
